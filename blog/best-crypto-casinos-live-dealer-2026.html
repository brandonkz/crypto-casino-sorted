Provably Fair in Live Dealer Games?

Here's the honest answer: live dealer games cannot be provably fair in the way crypto-native games like Dice, Crash, or Plinko are. And that's okay — here's why.

Provably fair works by generating outcomes through cryptographic hashes that players can verify after each round. It's perfect for RNG games where a computer determines the result. But live dealer games use physical objects — real cards shuffled by a real shuffling machine, real roulette wheels with real balls. You can't hash a physical card.

Instead, live dealer games rely on a different trust model:

  • Regulated studios — Evolution, Pragmatic, and Ezugi studios are licensed and audited by gambling regulators.
  • 24/7 surveillance — Every table is monitored by cameras, pit bosses, and automated card-reading technology.
  • Third-party audits — Independent testing agencies like GLI and eCOGRA verify game fairness.
  • Optical recognition — Cards and wheel positions are read by cameras and verified in real-time against the software.

Is it as trustless as provably fair? No. But the multi-layered oversight from providers, regulators, and testing labs makes live dealer games extremely difficult to rig. The providers have far more to lose from a rigging scandal than they'd ever gain from cheating individual players.
