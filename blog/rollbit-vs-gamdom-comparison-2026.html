The RLB Token vs Rain: Two Ways to Return Value

This is the core philosophical difference between these two platforms, and it's worth understanding before you deposit anywhere.

Rollbit's RLB token is a buy-and-burn mechanism. A percentage of Rollbit's house edge profits is used to buy RLB tokens from the open market and burn them permanently — reducing supply over time. If you hold RLB, you're betting on Rollbit's long-term volume growth translating into sustained token appreciation. It turned a lot of early adopters into genuine winners. The downside: token value is volatile, holding is speculative, and there's no direct "cashback" from your own losses — you're just hoping the house burns enough tokens to push your bag higher.

Gamdom's Rain is different. It's not a token — it's a live chat mechanic. Gamdom distributes random crypto drops ("Rain") to active users in the chat. The more active you are, the higher your probability of getting rained on. Rain is funded by Gamdom's profits and distributed continuously throughout the day. It's not life-changing money — typical drops are a few cents to a dollar or two — but it's genuinely random money falling into your account for doing nothing except being present.

The honest comparison: RLB is for investors who believe in Rollbit's long-term volume. Rain is for recreational gamblers who enjoy the community and want small but consistent cashback. These are fundamentally different value propositions — neither is strictly "better."

Original Games: Rollbit's X50 vs Gamdom's HiLo

Both platforms have signature provably fair games that aren't available elsewhere. This is where the real differentiation lives.

Rollbit X50

X50 is a crash game with a multiplier that rises from 1x and can "crash" at any point. The house edge is 1% — low for the genre. What makes X50 distinctive is the smooth multiplier curve and the way it integrates with the live Rollbit community, where streamers and chat participants react to crashes in real time. The 1% house edge on X50 is competitive with the best crash games in the industry.

Rollbit also has RollBot — a proprietary dice game — and their NFT slot system (more on that below).

Gamdom HiLo

HiLo is a card prediction game — guess whether the next card will be higher or lower than the current one. Simple concept, provably fair, and with flexible bet sizing from dust to whale territory. The house edge varies by configuration but typically sits around 1.5–2% at standard settings, higher if you're aggressive with your guesses.

Gamdom also has Roulette, Crash, and Mines variants, all provably fair. Their Crash game is solid — nothing groundbreaking, but reliable and well-implemented.

Winner: Rollbit — X50's 1% house edge and deep community integration give it an edge over HiLo. But if card-based games are your preference, Gamdom's HiLo is genuinely entertaining.

The mechanic uses real on-chain NFTs from popular collections, generating a slot result based on the NFT's traits. The house edge on NFT Slots ranges from 1–4% depending on configuration.

Is this gimmick or genuine innovation? Honestly, a bit of both. If you hold NFTs from supported collections, it's a fascinating way to put them to work.
