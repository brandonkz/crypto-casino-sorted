2. The "Rakeback" Clarification: 5% of What, Exactly?

This distinction matters more than any other in this guide, and most sites gloss over it. Rollbit's rakeback is calculated as a percentage of the house edge on your bet — not a percentage of your bet amount, and not a percentage of your session loss.

Example — $100 bet on a dice game with 1% house edge
House edge on bet = $100 × 1% = $1.00
5% base rakeback = $1.00 × 5% = $0.05 returned
Effective house edge after rakeback = 1% × (1 − 5%) = 0.95%

You bet $100. You get $0.05 back. Not $5.
Example — $100 bet on a slot with 4% house edge
House edge on bet = $100 × 4% = $4.00
5% base rakeback = $4.00 × 5% = $0.20 returned
Effective house edge after rakeback = 4% × (1 − 5%) = 3.8%

Across $10,000 in total wagering on a 4% house-edge game: the casino's expected take is $400. The 5% rakeback returns $20 of that $400 back to you. Not $500. The house still has a large edge — rakeback makes the game cheaper over time, but it does not make it profitable.

What "5% rakeback" is not: It is not a 5% rebate on your deposit. It is not 5% of your losses returned. It is 5% of the mathematical house advantage on each individual bet. On low-edge provably fair games (e.g. 1% Dice), the absolute dollar return per bet is very small. On high-edge slots (some run 4-8% RTP gaps), the absolute return is larger, but so is your expected loss.
