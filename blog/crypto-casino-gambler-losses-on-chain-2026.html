Important Caveats

⚠️ Why the real numbers might be different

Before you share this article screaming "casinos keep 99% of deposits!", here's what our methodology doesn't capture:

  • Unlabeled withdrawal wallets. Casinos often use different hot wallets for payouts than for deposits. If we don't have their withdrawal wallet addresses, those payouts won't show up in our data. This likely explains why some casinos show near-100% retention.
  • Token withdrawals. We're primarily tracking native ETH transfers. Withdrawals in USDT, USDC, or other ERC-20 tokens would be missed in this analysis.
  • Multi-chain withdrawals. A user might deposit on Ethereum but withdraw on BSC, Polygon, or Tron. We're only tracking Ethereum here.
  • Sample bias. We're looking at wallets from the last 200 transactions per casino. This skews toward recent, active depositors who may not have withdrawn yet.

That said — even if withdrawals are 5-10x higher than what we captured, the data still paints a grim picture for gamblers. And the deposits are accurate: those transactions are on-chain, immutable, and verifiable.

What the Data Actually Tells Us

The raw numbers are extreme, but the directional truth is clear:

1. Most gamblers never withdraw. Whether it's because they lose everything, chase losses, or abandon wallets — the overwhelming pattern is money flowing in one direction: into the casino.

2. Whales lose the most. The top 10 losers account for $76 million in deposits. These aren't casual players. They're high-rollers burning through millions, often across multiple casinos.

3. The house edge is real and compounding. Even if a casino's theoretical house edge is 1-5%, the effective loss rate is much higher because players keep gambling until they lose. A 2% edge per bet becomes a 100% loss if you play long enough.

4. Crypto makes it frictionless. No bank holds, no cooling-off periods, no wife seeing the credit card statement. Depositing 100 ETH ($200K+) takes 30 seconds. That's a feature for the casino, not the player.
